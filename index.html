From Signal
to System

Fusing traditional analysis, data-driven methods, and artificial intelligence for the intelligence warfighting function.

OSINT is the vehicle, not the subject. The subject is sound tradecraft, measured against the characteristics of effective intelligence in FM 2-0 (2026), and the disciplined integration of data and AI into that tradecraft. One scenario, Exercise NORTH WATCH, runs through all three modules and builds toward a working tool you host yourself.

Overarching goals

By the end of the OPD you will be able to:

  • Frame an intelligence problem into requirements, indicators, and a collection focus, and defend that framing against the characteristics of effective intelligence.
  • Collect, evaluate, and structure live open-source data (news, flight, maritime, conflict-event, and GNSS-interference streams) while managing overload and rating source reliability.
  • Apply structured analytic techniques to separate fact from assumption, test hypotheses, and express calibrated confidence.
  • Integrate free AI tools (search, summarization, entity extraction, translation, drafting) as augmentation, then verify and red-team their outputs.
  • Design, build, document, and host a working analyst-support or AI tool on GitHub with proper OPSEC, oversight, and data-rights safeguards.
  • Produce and brief a fused product that meets every characteristic and states where AI was used and how it was checked.

The standard: characteristics of effective intelligence

Every discussion, exercise, and product is graded against this rubric. AI and data tools change how fast and how completely you meet it; they do not change the standard, and several of these are exactly where naive AI use fails. Source: FM 2-0, Intelligence (October 2026), paragraphs 1-3 and 1-4. The first six are the characteristics of relevant information (ADP 3-13); the last three are specific to intelligence.

Relevant-information characteristics

Accurate
Identifies threat intentions, capabilities, limitations, and dispositions as far as possible. Draws on multiple sources and disciplines to reduce the risk of deception or misreading, and presents alternative or contradictory assessments when possible.
Timely
Arrives early enough to support operations and prevent surprise, and flows continuously before, during, and after an operation.
Usable
Comes in the correct data-file format for databasing and display, so it can feed further analysis and be used across the staff.
Complete
Products and briefings contain every necessary component.
Precise
Gives the necessary detail, with enough clarity to answer the requirement.
Reliable
Evaluates how far the collected information is trustworthy, uncorrupted, and undistorted. Any concern about reliability is stated up front.

Intelligence-centric characteristics

Relevant
Supports the commander's requirements.
Predictive
Tells the commander what the threat can do (emphasizing the most dangerous COA) and is most likely to do (the most likely COA), and anticipates the commander's intelligence needs.
Tailored
Delivered in the format the commander and staff asked for: clear, concise, and usable for a decision.

Doctrinal anchor: FM 2-0 (2026) and this course

The October 2026 FM 2-0 speaks directly to what this OPD practices. Read these four points before Module 01; the discussions come back to them.

Intelligence process

FM 2-0 now uses the joint step names: plan and direct, collect, process and exploit, analyze and produce, disseminate and integrate, evaluate and provide feedback. The steps often overlap rather than run in strict sequence. Each module covers two of them.

AI/ML and data literacy

Processing may integrate AI/ML to structure and refine raw data, and FM 2-0 expects automated processing to grow. Analysts must therefore be data literate: able to read, understand, and evaluate data, and aware of its limitations, threats, and risks.

PED vs. analysis and exploitation

PED will lean more and more on AI/ML to structure and fuse data. Analysis and exploitation can be accelerated by those tools, but FM 2-0 says its critical thinking and contextual understanding "fundamentally require human judgment." That line is the thesis of this course.

Human-machine teaming

Exploitation uses human-machine teaming to produce multidiscipline intelligence (the term FM 2-0 now uses in place of all-source). Information must be evaluated and corroborated for reliability, validity, and relevance before it reaches a product.

The Running Scenario

Exercise North Watch

Indications & warning on grey-zone threats to critical undersea infrastructure in the Baltic Sea.

Your cell supports a multinational task force responsible for I&W on threats to undersea cables and pipelines. The commander's guidance: "I do not want to be surprised by the next cable incident. Tell me what to watch and what would tell us something is about to happen."

Why this is real

After repeated cable and pipeline incidents, NATO launched the Baltic Sentry vigilance activity in January 2025 to protect critical undersea infrastructure. In parallel, an allied monitoring system applies AI to vessel data, including the AIS positions ships broadcast, to score the risk posed by each vessel entering an area of interest. That production system is, in effect, the full-scale version of the tool you will build in miniature by Session 3. Several real incidents in this problem set were variously judged deliberate, ruled accidental, or left unattributable. That ambiguity is the point, and it is where bias, overattribution, and the line between fact and assumption show up. Source: NATO SHAPE · Baltic Sentry.

The streams you will fuse

Maritime / AIS

Shadow-fleet vessels loitering near cable corridors, going dark, or behaving anomalously.

Air / ADS-B

Patrol and anomalous air activity, and the reality that military aircraft often do not broadcast, which teaches the limits of any one feed.

GNSS interference

GPS jamming and spoofing in the region, observable through public interference maps.

Events / reporting

News streams and conflict-event data covering incidents, enforcement actions, and airspace violations.

Module 01

The Analyst & the Flood

Traditional tradecraft, cognition, and the data-overload problem.

Before automation, judgment. This module makes the analyst's mind the first instrument, frames the NORTH WATCH problem, and has you collect manually from live feeds so you personally feel the overload the rest of the program is built to solve.

Learning outcomes

  • Plan and direct: convert a commander's problem into intelligence requirements (a PIR broken into SIRs) and observable indicators.
  • Identify three biases that degrade analysis and a mitigation for each.
  • Collect from three live streams and rate each source's reliability.
  • Explain why "more collection" can degrade decision quality.
  • Produce a baseline assessment that separates fact from assumption and assigns confidence.

Required reading

Discussion

  1. Heuer argues analysts see what they expect to see. Where in NORTH WATCH are you most at risk of "knowing" the answer before the evidence supports it, and how would you guard against it?
  2. "Drowning in Data" calls overload a flaw in institutional design more than a technical problem. Do you agree? What in your formation's design makes overload worse?
  3. The article names hallucination, automation bias, and adversarial manipulation as new AI risks. Which worries you most for an OSINT cell, and why?
  4. The event cycle moves in hours and minutes; doctrine often moves in days and weeks. What breaks first when your analytic cycle is slower than the event cycle?
  5. Map each of FM 2-0's nine characteristics of effective intelligence to NORTH WATCH. Which is hardest to meet with open sources alone?
  6. FM 2-0 says the intelligence process steps often overlap rather than run in sequence. Where in your Phase 1 work did plan and direct and collect blur together, and was that a problem?
  7. When is collecting less the right call, and how would you justify that to a commander who equates more reporting with more security?

Practical exercise · Phase 1: Frame and collect

Hand-off › Keep the framed problem, the collection log, and the baseline. Module 02 structures and analyzes them.

Check on learning

Define PIR/SIR/indicator. Name three biases and a mitigation for each. State the reliability scale. Name the six intelligence process steps. Then grade your baseline against the rubric — the bar this module is accurate, reliable, and complete: did you separate fact from assumption, rate sources, and avoid overattribution?

Module 02

Augmenting the Analyst

Data-driven methods, structured analytic techniques, and AI as a teammate.

Now discipline the flood. Take the messy data from Module 01 and apply data structuring, structured analytic techniques, and free AI tools as augmentation, then verify and red-team everything the AI produces.

Learning outcomes

  • Process and exploit: structure unstructured reporting into a consistent schema (who, what, where, when, source, reliability), applying FM 2-0's data-literacy standard.
  • Distinguish PED, where AI/ML increasingly structures and fuses data, from analysis and exploitation, where human judgment is required.
  • Use AI for search, summarization, and entity extraction, and state the limitation of each.
  • Run an Analysis of Competing Hypotheses on one incident, using AI as devil's advocate without surrendering judgment.
  • Analyze and produce: verify AI outputs against sources, detect hallucination, and state likelihood and confidence to the ICD 203 standard.
  • Compare a human-only product to an AI-augmented one against the characteristics.

Required reading

Discussion

  1. The AI-supported group produced clearly better assessments under time pressure and probability estimates closer to experts, yet reported no higher confidence in their sources or analysis. Why might better results not raise confidence, and is that a feature or a bug for a professional?
  2. Of the three functions (search, summarization, entity recognition), which is most dangerous to trust blindly in NORTH WATCH, and what does its failure look like?
  3. The authors flag data protection and confidentiality as central. What is the bright line you will never cross with a public AI tool? (Anchor to Guardrails.)
  4. Brown frames trust in AI as something to be developed. What would have to be true about a tool before you let its output into a decision product?
  5. Where does ACH protect you from the biases Heuer described? Where could AI quietly reintroduce them?
  6. FM 2-0 separates PED, which will "increasingly rely on" AI/ML, from analysis and exploitation, whose critical thinking and contextual understanding "fundamentally require human judgment." In your Phase 2 work, which AI step was PED and which crossed into analysis? Did you notice when it crossed?
  7. ICD 203 requires analysts to describe the quality and credibility of their sources and to express uncertainty properly. Which of its standards did your AI-augmented assessment fall short on first?
  8. Re-grade your Module 01 product now that it is structured. Which characteristic improved most from structuring alone, before any AI? (FM 2-0's usable is a likely answer: data in a consistent format.)

Practical exercise · Phase 2: Structure, augment, verify

Hand-off › Keep the structured table, the verified entity set, the ACH matrix, and the comparison. This is the engine your Module 03 tool will automate.

Check on learning

Name the three AI functions and a limitation of each. State Heuer's eight ACH steps (see Templates). Express one judgment with both a likelihood term and a separate confidence level. Define hallucination and automation bias with a NORTH WATCH example of each. Did you catch at least one AI error? Explain the difference between PED and analysis and exploitation. The augmented product should be more timely and complete without becoming less accurate or reliable.

Module 03

Building the Machine

Automation, computerized support tools, and the GitHub capstone.

Turn the repeatable parts of the workflow into a tool you own. Build and host a working analyst-support or AI tool on GitHub, then produce and brief a final fused I&W product. This is where the OPD becomes a capability you keep.

Learning outcomes

  • Decide which steps to automate and which must stay human, and justify the line.
  • Build a working tool at your chosen track that automates collection, structuring, or summarization.
  • Host it on GitHub with a clear README and an OPSEC/data-rights statement another analyst could follow.
  • Produce a final fused I&W product covering most likely and most dangerous COAs.
  • Disseminate and integrate: brief the tool and product, stating where AI was used, how it was verified, and what the tool cannot do.
  • Evaluate and provide feedback: collect peer feedback on the product and the tool, and record what you would change.

Required reading

Discussion

  1. The support-tools reading is about aiding, not replacing, analysts. Where did you keep a human in the decision, and why there?
  2. What is the most useful action you would let an agent take unattended in NORTH WATCH, and the first action you would never automate?
  3. Your tool will eventually be wrong. What is its failure mode, who notices, and how fast? If you cannot answer, it is not ready for a real product.
  4. Re-read Brown after building. Would you trust your own tool in a product for a commander? What single change moves it from demo to trusted teammate?
  5. Which characteristic does your tool most improve (usually timely, usable, or complete), and which does it most endanger (usually accurate or reliable)? How does the design protect the one at risk?
  6. FM 2-0 says the Army "will likely implement fully in the near future" automated processing, and requires data to be visible, accessible, understandable, linked, trustworthy, interoperable, and secure. Which of those seven does your tool's output fail, and what would fix it?

Practical exercise · Phase 3: Ship it

Capstone standard › The repo is live and legible to another analyst; the README and OPSEC/data-rights statement are present and correct; the final product meets every characteristic, including a clear AI-use-and-verification line.
The Capstone

Three Build Tracks

Every cell hosts a tool on GitHub. Choose a track by skill and ambition. The grade is on analytic value and documentation, not code volume. No cell finishes without a live repo, but the bar scales to the cell.

Track 1 · No code

Link & feed board

A single static page on GitHub Pages that becomes the cell's watch board: curated labeled links, embedded public maps where permitted, a methodology note, and a log section.

Adds value: standardizes where you look and how you record (usable, complete). Version-controlled and sharable.

Track 2 · Light code

Notebook pipeline

A Colab or Jupyter notebook in a repo that pulls a free API (GDELT, OpenSky), filters to the area of interest, structures results into your schema, and optionally drafts a sourced summary a human verifies.

Adds value: automates collection and structuring (timely, usable) while a human owns the summary.

Track 3 · More code

Hosted app / mini-agent

A small Streamlit app on Streamlit Community Cloud, or a light pipeline, that ingests a feed, structures it, flags a transparent anomaly (e.g. loitering in a cable corridor), and presents a verifiable draft brief.

Adds value: end-to-end augmentation and the governance question of what the agent may and may not do unattended.

Hard rule for every track

Respect each source's terms of use. Do not re-serve another provider's raw licensed data, attribute every source, and keep everything unclassified and non-CUI. Prefer fully open feeds (e.g. GDELT) for anything public.

Mandatory

Guardrails

Brief this before any collection and reinforce it every session. These are part of the grade, not optional.

The one-line rule

If you would not put it on a public website with your name on it, do not put it in a public AI tool or a public repository.

Classification & AI hygiene

  • This OPD is unclassified and uses open sources only. Bring no classified or CUI into any exercise.
  • Never input classified or CUI into any public or commercial AI tool, search engine, or third-party service.
  • Treat anything typed into a public AI tool as potentially retained and reviewable. When in doubt, do not type it.

OPSEC for collection

  • Do not research from government accounts or systems or under your own name, and do not improvise your own cover accounts. Use only the managed-attribution capability and research accounts your command has approved, under DoDI 3115.18 and local policy. If your unit has none, stay with passive, low-signature viewing of public pages.
  • Avoid a signature: do not repeatedly query the same niche targets from an identifiable account.
  • This is about infrastructure, vessels, and events, not specific real persons.

Intelligence oversight

  • Comply with EO 12333 (as amended), DODD 5240.01, DODM 5240.01, AR 381-10, DODD 5148.13 (Intelligence Oversight), DoDI 3115.18 (DoD access to and use of publicly available information), Army Directive 2016-37 (U.S. Army Open-Source Intelligence Activities), and USAREUR-AF guidance. FM 2-0 (2026), appendix C, summarizes these authorities.
  • FM 2-0 requires a documented mission and authority for any intelligence activity, and intelligence oversight training before collecting. This OPD is training on publicly available information, not an intelligence collection mission; your command decides whether any part of it needs more authority.
  • Do not intentionally collect, retain, or disseminate information on U.S. persons except as authorities permit (DODM 5240.01, procedures 2 through 4).
  • Coordinate this OPD with your command's oversight and legal/SJA points of contact before execution. If unsure a task is permissible, stop and ask.

Data rights & the public repo

  • Free does not mean unrestricted. Respect every source's terms of use.
  • ACLED specifically: registered free use is fine, but do not re-serve raw data, build a substitute dashboard, or commit raw ACLED data to a public repo. Use must be transformative and attributed.
  • For public tools: no API keys, no raw licensed datasets, no sensitive content, clear attribution. Verify AI outputs, crowd maps, and machine-coded events before trusting them.

Templates

PIR / SIR / indicator

  • PIR (one decision-relevant question): e.g. "Will a vessel deliberately threaten undersea infrastructure in [sub-area] in the next 30 days?"
  • SIRs that decompose the PIR, each with observable indicators.
  • For each indicator, mark: open-source collectable? (Y/N). List what you want but cannot collect openly as a gap.

Likelihood & confidence

Likelihood and confidence are two different statements. Likelihood is how probable you judge the event to be. Confidence is how good your evidence and reasoning are. ICD 203 says not to combine the two in one sentence. Use the standard terms:

Likelihood termProbability
Almost no chance / remote01–05%
Very unlikely / highly improbable05–20%
Unlikely / improbable20–45%
Roughly even chance / roughly even odds45–55%
Likely / probable55–80%
Very likely / highly probable80–95%
Almost certain / nearly certain95–99%

Confidence (low / moderate / high) reflects source reliability, corroboration across independent streams, and how much rests on assumptions. Example: "A shadow-fleet tanker likely caused the cable break through anchor drag. We have moderate confidence because the AIS track and the damage timing agree, but the only evidence of intent is a single report."

Source: ODNI, Intelligence Community Directive 203, Analytic Standards.

ACH matrix

Heuer's eight steps (Psychology of Intelligence Analysis, ch. 8):

  1. Identify all reasonable hypotheses; brainstorm as a group (sabotage / anchor drag / innocent transit / other).
  2. List significant evidence and arguments, including assumptions and notable absences of evidence.
  3. Build the matrix: hypotheses across the top, evidence down the side. Mark each cell Consistent / Inconsistent / N/A and judge each item's diagnosticity.
  4. Refine: reword or add hypotheses, and drop evidence that does not help distinguish between them.
  5. Draw tentative conclusions by trying to disprove hypotheses; the one with the least inconsistent evidence is the working estimate.
  6. Test sensitivity: which few items does the conclusion hinge on, and what if they are wrong or deceptive?
  7. Report the relative likelihood of all hypotheses, not just the winner.
  8. Name indicators and milestones that would show events taking a different course.

AI-output verification checklist

  • Traced to a named source I can open.
  • Source reliability and credibility rated.
  • Marked confirmed / unsupported / hallucinated.
  • Entities (names, dates, vessels, places) spot-checked for tagging errors.
  • Alternative explanation considered (did the AI just confirm my bias?).
